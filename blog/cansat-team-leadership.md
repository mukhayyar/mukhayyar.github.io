# From 22nd to 11th: Leading a CanSat Team

In September 2024 I became team leader of EEPISAT. Previous year we placed 22nd. My goal: top 15.

## What Changed

### Structure
Split team into clear divisions: Hardware, Software, Science, Operations. Each had a lead who reported to me. Reduced communication overhead.

### Technical Focus
- Moved from Arduino to Teensy 4.1 (10x processing power)
- Built portable GCS on Raspberry Pi instead of laptop
- Implemented PID control for paraglider steering

### Partnerships
Cold-emailed 20+ companies. PT Pasifik Satelit Nusantara (PSN) responded. They funded our competition trip and gave us study tours at their satellite facilities.

## The Competition

CanSat Competition USA is hosted by American Astronautical Society. University teams worldwide design, build, and fly a satellite that fits in a soda can.

Our 2025 mission: autonomous paragliding descent with multispectral imaging.

## Result

**11th place worldwide.** 11 spots up from previous year.

Also got:
- Teknofest 2025 finalist (Top 15) — our first-ever entry
- Top 4 at Indonesia Aerospace Hackathon with our CanSat Kit
- Invited to present at APRSAF-30 in Perth, Australia

## What I Learned

Leadership in technical teams isn't about being the best engineer. It's about:

1. **Removing blockers** — buy components before they're needed
2. **Setting clear goals** — "top 15" is measurable, "do our best" isn't
3. **Trusting people** — let leads make decisions
4. **Saying no** — we rejected 3 feature requests that would've delayed launch

The team is still going. They'll beat 11th next year.
